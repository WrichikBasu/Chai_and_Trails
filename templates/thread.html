Planning to ride Manali to Kaza in the first week of June with a friend on a second bike. Both of us have done Ladakh once, neither of us has done Spiti.

What I think I know so far:

  • Gramphu to Batal is the part that decides the day. Everything I read says four hours minimum, six if the nullahs are running.
  • Water crossings peak in the afternoon, so Batal by noon or wait it out.
  • Fuel at Manali, then nothing serious until Kaza.

Questions I have not been able to answer: is the Chhota Dara stretch graded this year, and is there any point carrying a spare 5 litre can if we are not detouring to Chandratal?

Slow is a speed, not a failure.

Meera Iyer said:

is the Chhota Dara stretch graded this year

Partly. The BRO worked on it through last September and it held up over winter, but the section right after Chhota Dara goes back to loose rock every June because of the melt. Expect it to be rideable, not comfortable.

Carry the spare fuel anyway. Not for distance, for the day you sit at a nullah for three hours with the engine idling and then take the long way round. Five litres strapped low is worth the nuisance.

Timing

Leave Manali by 5 AM. Not 6. The queue at the tunnel and the crossings both punish a late start, and you want to be past Batal before the water comes up.

Adding one thing Tenzin is too polite to say: first week of June is early. It is a fine week to ride if the season opened on time, and a wasted week if it did not.

Keep the plan, but check the road status thread the day before you leave and be ready to flip the direction and come in through Shimla instead. That side opens earlier and the altitude gain is gentler, which your friend will thank you for.

If the map shows a shortcut, ask a local first.

Did this exact route last June on an Interceptor, which is the wrong bike for it, so take the following as a warning rather than advice.

The crossings are not the problem. The problem is the hour after, when everything on the bike is wet and you are still at 4,000 metres losing light. Dry socks in a sealed bag, and stop earlier than you think you need to.
